## **A/B Testing Strategy**

### **Subject Line Tests**

| Test             | Variant A         | Variant B          | Variant C        |
| ---------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ---------------- |
| **Pattern type** | Question          | Statement          | Quote            |
| **Specificity**  | Dollar amount     | Time amount        | Specific story   |
| **Tone**         | Urgency/risk      | Opportunity/gain   | Curiosity        |
| **Length**       | Short (3-5 words) | Medium (6-8 words) | Long (10+ words) |

### **Body Copy Tests**

| Element           | Test A                 | Test B                 | Test C            |
| ----------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Opening**       | Personal story         | Statistics             | Direct question   |
| **Proof type**    | Specific advisor quote | Before/after breakdown | Comparison table  |
| **CTA placement** | Early (paragraph 3)    | Late (end)             | Middle + end      |
| **Reply ask**     | Open question          | Multiple choice        | Specific scenario |

### **Send Time Tests**

- **Morning:** 8:00-9:00 AM (before client meetings)
- **Midday:** 12:00-1:00 PM (lunch break)
- **Evening:** 5:00-6:00 PM (end of day)

### **Success Metrics**

1. **Demo page visits** (primary)
2. **Reply rate** (feedback quality)
3. **Demo time on page** (engagement depth)
4. **Click-through rate by step** (see where drop-off happens)
5. **Unsubscribe rate** (tone calibration)

### **Testing Protocol**

**Week 1-2: Subject Line Optimization**

- Test Step 1 variants (A/B/C with 33% each)
- Winner moves to full sequence

**Week 3-4: Body Copy Testing**

- Test Step 2 body variants
- Measure reply quality, not just quantity

**Week 5-6: CTA Placement**

- Test early vs. late CTA
- Measure demo page time as quality indicator

**Week 7+: Optimize by Step**

- Identify highest drop-off step
- A/B test new variant for that specific step

## **Key Principles Applied**

### **From Hormozi / Modern Email Marketing:**

1. **Pattern Interrupt Subjects** - Curiosity gaps instead of "quick question"
2. **Specificity Over Abstraction** - "$400/hour typo" not "time savings"
3. **Lead With Their Pain** - Every email opens with THEIR problem
4. **One CTA Per Email** - Demo link only. No calendar links competing.
5. **Progressive Disclosure** - Hook → Proof → Education → Case Study → Objections → Ask → Exit
6. **Remove Passive Language** - No "just", "quick", "wondering"
7. **Concrete Examples** - Sarah in California, $30K mistake
8. **Graceful Exit** - Permission-based close
9. **Reply-Friendly CTAs** - Low friction "hit reply"
10. **Handle Objections Before The Ask** - Remove barriers in Step 5

### **Psychological Triggers:**

- **Loss aversion** (Step 1C, Step 3A, Step 5B)
- **Social proof** (Step 2A, 2C, Step 4)
- **Authority** (Step 3A - "we analyzed 200+ reports")
- **Reciprocity** (Step 6 - offering checklist/templates)
- **Scarcity** (Step 6C - real constraint, not fake urgency)
- **Consistency** (Step 6 - "building with actual advisor input")
- **Risk reversal** (Step 5B - "I'll buy you coffee")

## **Follow-Up Strategy for Repliers**

**If they reply with feedback:**

1. Respond within 4 hours with genuine thank you
2. Send promised resource (checklist/templates)
3. Ask one follow-up question to keep conversation going
4. After 2-3 exchanges, soft pitch: "Want to see the beta?"

**If they click demo but don't reply:**

- Wait 5 days
- Send single follow-up: "Saw you checked out the demo. One question..."
- If no response, add to monthly newsletter list

**If they visit pricing/sign-up but don't convert:**

- Send "Questions about getting started?" email
- Offer 15-min demo call
- Address specific objections
