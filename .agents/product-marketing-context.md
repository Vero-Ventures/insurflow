# Product Marketing Context

_Last updated: 2026-03-16_

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** InsurFlow helps Canadian consumers understand how much term life insurance they may need and connect with the best-fit provider through one simple online journey.
**What it does:** InsurFlow is a consumer-facing term life insurance broker experience for the Canadian market. It guides people through a short intake, shows a clear non-binding estimate, and moves them into a streamlined application flow so they can be matched with a suitable insurance provider without dealing with confusing jargon or fragmented broker handoffs.
**Product category:** Online term life insurance broker / digital insurance marketplace
**Product type:** Consumer fintech / insurance marketplace
**Business model:** Broker-style insurance distribution for Canadian term life consumers; revenue model not yet finalized in this document, but positioning assumes provider-side economics rather than consumer subscription pricing.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Not applicable; B2C product.
**Decision-makers:** Canadian consumers buying or comparing term life insurance for themselves or their family.
**Primary use case:** A consumer wants a fast, understandable way to explore and apply for term life insurance without being overwhelmed by insurance terminology or forced into a slow offline process.
**Jobs to be done:**

- Help me figure out whether I need term life insurance and roughly how much coverage makes sense.
- Help me compare my options without doing all the legwork myself.
- Help me apply with confidence and understand what happens next.
  **Use cases:**
- First-time term life shoppers who do not know where to start.
- Parents, partners, or homeowners who want to protect dependents.
- Canadians who want digital convenience instead of a traditional broker meeting.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Buying term life insurance feels confusing, slow, and hard to trust.
**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Traditional broker experiences can feel opaque and manual.
- Carrier sites make consumers learn insurance language too early.
- Generic quote flows often do not explain what the numbers mean.
  **What it costs them:** Lost time, indecision, delayed coverage, and low confidence in whether they chose the right provider.
  **Emotional tension:** Consumers feel uncertainty, skepticism, and avoidance around a product that matters to their family.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Digital-first insurance apps and broker experiences such as Lemonade-like insurance journeys or online broker funnels — often not tailored to the Canadian term life use case or not focused on making term life selection feel simple.
**Secondary:** Traditional insurance brokers and advisor-led life insurance processes — fall short because they are slower, less transparent, and harder to self-serve.
**Indirect:** Doing nothing, delaying the decision, or piecing together advice from carrier websites and search results — falls short because consumers still do not know what to buy or who fits them best.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Canada-first consumer experience
- Plain-language intake and estimate flow
- Broker positioning without forcing the user through a traditional broker workflow
- Provider matching inside one guided product journey
  **How we do it differently:** We combine needs-estimate guidance, simple application flow, and provider connection into one digital consumer experience.
  **Why that's better:** Consumers get clarity and momentum without having to become insurance experts first.
  **Why customers choose us:** They want a faster, easier, more transparent way to get term life insurance in Canada.

## Objections

| Objection                                | Response                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "I do not know if I even need this."     | Start with education and a non-binding estimate that explains what coverage could look like before asking for a full commitment.                              |
| "I do not trust online insurance flows." | Emphasize plain language, clear next steps, privacy, and the fact that InsurFlow helps connect them with a real provider rather than acting like a black box. |
| "I can just talk to a broker later."     | Position InsurFlow as the faster way to get clarity first, then connect to the best-fit provider with less back-and-forth.                                    |

**Anti-persona:** Users looking for complex whole-life planning, wealth planning, or corporate insurance structures are not the v1 target.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Confusing jargon, slow forms, lack of transparency, and unclear provider fit in existing insurance shopping experiences.
**Pull:** A fast Canada-first term life journey that feels simple, modern, and understandable.
**Habit:** People delay life insurance or assume the process will be tedious.
**Anxiety:** Worry about sharing personal information, making the wrong decision, or being sold something they do not understand.

## Customer Language

**How they describe the problem:**

- "I just want to know what I need without talking to five different people."
- "I do not understand all the insurance terminology."
- "I want the best option, but I do not know how to compare providers."
  **How they describe us:**
- "A simpler way to shop for term life insurance in Canada."
- "Like a modern broker, but easier to use online."
  **Words to use:** simple, clear, guided, compare, best-fit provider, Canada-first, non-binding estimate, term life insurance
  **Words to avoid:** advisor handoff, wealth planning, complex underwriting language, black-box, opaque, bespoke
  **Glossary:**
  | Term | Meaning |
  |------|---------|
  | term life insurance | Life insurance that covers a defined number of years |
  | non-binding estimate | An early estimate that helps orient the user, not a final offer |
  | provider | The insurance company or carrier that may offer the policy |
